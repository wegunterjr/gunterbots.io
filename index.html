Mission 1: The Robot's Heartbeat

Every robot you'll ever meet is a loop. Make a heart blink on a pretend micro:bit. That's the same idea as a rover's heartbeat.

On a computer

  1. Open makecode.microbit.org and start a new project.
  2. Inside forever, add show icon (a heart), pause 500 ms, clear screen, pause 500 ms.
  3. Watch the board on the left beat. That's a loop: do this, then do it again.

No robot required. The picture is the robot until a box arrives.

No computer handy

  1. You write the program. A crewmate (sibling, friend, anyone) is the robot.
  2. You say: “Clap. Wait. Clap. Wait. Repeat.” They loop until you say stop.
  3. Change the program: two claps, then a stomp. Switch jobs and go again.

That's the pirate way: steal the idea with whatever's on the table.

The course

A year of missions in four seasons

Each season is 12 weeks plus a catch-up week. A short mission takes a week; a bigger build, like a Line Lab, can take two. Every mission has a Rookie build and a Pro build. New members can join any season. Join mid-year and a short catch-up mission gets you moving.

Hardware

Year 1 kit: a micro:bit and a small car.

The kit is about $50–60: a BBC micro:bit V2 (~$16–26) plus an Elecfreaks Cutebot (~$30). That includes the board. Three AAA batteries from the grocery store. Buy it from the maker — we don't stock or ship kits. Tuition is the club.

That covers driving, lights, sonar, radio, and line-follow missions. Listings move, so we treat $80 as the ceiling.

Cutebot car · micro:bit V2 · Maqueen with micro:bit (~$54)

Not in the box: two small servos for the Cat Laser Turret (~$5, optional) and other add-ons listed per mission. Never required to finish the week.

Year 1 path

MakeCode + micro:bit

Blocks in the browser, then the same code on the Cutebot. This is the club's main track. Programming, driving, and sensors all run in the MakeCode simulator until the box arrives. A real rover on the floor or a tape track needs the kit; every mission also runs in the simulator.

Later path

Wokwi + ESP32

When you're ready for text code and a different chip, we use Wokwi in the browser, then a real ESP32 if you want one. Same missions, new brain. Year 1 stays on micro:bit so nobody buys two kits in October.

AI Lab

Once a month, the mission becomes an AI Lab

Kids learn three things: how to use AI as a helper, how to use it safely, and how to train their own models and put them on a robot. Free tools. No extra kit in year one.

Use it

AI as a helper

Ask it to explain code or find a bug. Then test what it says on the real robot. The robot is the judge.

Use it safely

Robot Rules for AI

What not to share, how to spot wrong answers, and why AI isn't a friend. The crew signs the Robot Rules. A grown-up stays in the room on chatbot missions because of age limits — the kid still drives.

Train it

Build your own model

Wave, clap, stand still — then drive the rover with the model you trained. Bad data makes bad AI. Kids see it.

Robot Rules for AI

  1. Never share your name, address, school, photos, or passwords with an AI.
  2. AI can be confidently wrong. Test it before you trust it.
  3. Chatbots have minimum ages. Stay in the room on those missions.
  4. AI is a tool, not a friend. Real questions go to real people.
  5. Say when AI helped. It's part of showing your work.
  6. You are the engineer. AI makes suggestions, you make decisions.

How kids use chatbots here

  • Kids never need a chatbot account. Model training uses free tools with no sign-up.
  • Chatbot missions can be demoed live in office hours so the kid still drives.
  • Accounts sit with the grown-up who pays. No private messages between members.
  • Showcase posts are approved before anyone else sees them. Office hours are recorded.

Questions

Before you join

Do we need a robot on day one? ▾

Programming, driving, and sensors run in the free MakeCode simulator — no kit. Physical movement (a rover on the floor, a tape track) needs the Year 1 kit. The kit is about $50–60: micro:bit V2 plus a Cutebot (or Maqueen with board ~$54), bought from the maker, batteries extra. Soil sensors, extra servos, and the Cat Laser Turret parts are optional add-ons, never required to finish the week.

What ages is this for? ▾

Kids 8–14 on Rookie, older kids and adults on Pro. Same weekly mission, two difficulty levels. Grown-ups who want to build too are welcome on Pro — same crew, same Thursday.

How much time is this, really? ▾

Plan on 1–2 hours of building during the week, plus a 10–15 minute Monday video. Inventor office hour is Thursday, drop-in, 60–90 minutes. The kid can skip a week and catch up from the library.

Do live sessions need a parent? ▾

Explorers have none — Monday video and a build at home. Inventor Thursday is optional drop-in help. The kid comes on with the robot. A grown-up does not need to sit in or teach. For kids under 13, someone should be in the house. Sessions are recorded.

How do you keep kids safe? ▾

The grown-up who pays creates and manages the accounts. There are no private messages between members. Showcase posts are approved before they appear. Office hours are recorded. For kids under 13 we collect only what we need, with parent consent (US COPPA). Chatbot missions can be demoed in office hours so the kid still drives; a grown-up stays nearby because of age limits.

Founding families open late October 2026. Public launch follows in November, in time for holiday gift subscriptions. Join the waitlist and you'll get the free 5-day Robot Starter Challenge — no kit needed — while we finish Season 1.

Monthly, a season, or summer — what's the difference? ▾

Monthly is the ongoing club, billed each month. Pause any month. A season is 12 weeks of missions plus a catch-up week — three months, paid once. Fall is September–November. Spring is January–March. Inventor includes one Thursday office hour each of those 12 mission weeks. Explorer season is $117 ($39 × 3). Inventor season is $297 ($99 × 3). Same dollars as three monthly bills; pick monthly if you might pause. Summer is Robot Rescue Camp, four weeks in June or July.

Can we use homeschool or ESA funds? ▾

Inventor at $297 a season is sized for family education accounts: about 7.5% of a Utah Fits All $4,000 award. Arizona ESA, Florida PEP, and Oklahoma's parental-choice tax credit list tutoring and online instruction as eligible categories. We are not automatically an approved vendor. Ask your program before you spend, and keep the kit (~$50–60) as a separate materials line.
