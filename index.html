The idea

Real hardware on the kitchen table. A mission every week.

Parents buy it because it builds thinking skills. Kids stay because they get to make a robot do something. A live roboticist shows up once a week to get everyone unstuck.

Primary

Parents of kids 8–14

Homeschool families, STEM-curious parents, and families whose school has no robotics team. Utah first, then anywhere with a kitchen table.

Also

Kids at heart

Adults who always wanted to build robots, retirees, grandparents building side by side. Same missions, Pro difficulty.

A roboticist teaching

The course

A year of missions in four seasons

Each season is 12 weeks plus a catch-up week. Every mission has a Rookie build and a Pro build. New members can join any season. Join mid-year and a short catch-up mission gets you moving.

Hardware path

One kit. The whole year.

A micro:bit V2 robot car (Elecfreaks Cutebot or DFRobot Maqueen) in the $40–80 range. We link the kit on signup. Every mission also runs in the free MakeCode simulator, so families can start before the box arrives.

Why it stays fun

Every mission ends with something to show.

A robot that dances, escapes a maze, or guards the cookie jar. Showing off is the hook. Badges, the monthly Build-Off, and the quarterly Robot Expo give everyone a reason to finish.

AI Lab

Once a month, the mission becomes an AI Lab

Kids learn three things: how to use AI as a helper, how to use it safely, and how to train their own models and put them on a robot. Free tools. No extra kit in year one.

Use it

AI as a helper

Ask it to explain code or find a bug. Then test what it says on the real robot. The robot is the judge.

Use it safely

Robot Rules for AI

What not to share, how to spot wrong answers, and why AI isn't a friend. Parents sign the rules with their kid.

Train it

Build your own model

Wave, clap, stand still — then drive the rover with the model you trained. Bad data makes bad AI. Kids see it.

Robot Rules for AI

  1. Never share your name, address, school, photos, or passwords with an AI.
  2. AI can be confidently wrong. Test it before you trust it.
  3. Use chatbots with a grown-up. Many have minimum ages.
  4. AI is a tool, not a friend. Real questions go to real people.
  5. Say when AI helped. It's part of showing your work.
  6. You are the engineer. AI makes suggestions, you make decisions.

How kids use chatbots here

  • Kids never need a chatbot account. Model training uses free tools with no sign-up.
  • Chatbot missions are done with a parent, or demoed live in office hours.
  • Parents own every account. No private messages between members.
  • Showcase posts are approved before anyone else sees them. Live sessions are recorded.

Questions

Before you join

Do we need a robot on day one? ▾

No. Every mission runs in the free MakeCode simulator at makecode.microbit.org. When you're ready, buy one recommended micro:bit V2 robot car ($40–80). We link the kit. We don't ship parts ourselves.

What ages is this for? ▾

Kids 8–14 on Rookie, older kids and adults on Pro. Same weekly mission, two difficulty levels. Grandparents and parents who want to build beside a kid are welcome — that's a lot of Inventor members.

How much time is this, really? ▾

Plan on 1–2 hours of building during the week, plus a 10–15 minute Monday video. Inventor office hour is Thursday, 60–90 minutes. You can skip a week and catch up from the library.

How do you keep kids safe? ▾

Parents create and manage the accounts. There are no private messages between members. Showcase posts are approved before they appear. Live sessions are recorded. For kids under 13 we collect only what we need, with parent consent (US COPPA). Chatbot missions are done with a grown-up.

When does it start? ▾

Founding families open late October 2026. Public launch follows in November, in time for holiday gift subscriptions. Join the waitlist and you'll get the free 5-day Robot Starter Challenge — no kit needed — while we finish Season 1.

Head to the course at learn.mgit.io and log in with the account your parent created.
